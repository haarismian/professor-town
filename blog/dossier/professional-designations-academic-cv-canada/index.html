Professional Designations on an Academic CV: Where They Go

By Haaris Mian September 4, 2026 9 min read

You have a CPA, a CFA charter, or a P.Eng., fifteen years of real work behind it, and a Canadian university job posting open in another tab. You have no idea where professional designations go on an academic CV: after your name, under Education, in a section of their own, or nowhere at all because academics will think it looks like a sales resume.

This post is about retrievability during Canadian sessional and teaching-stream screens: the July staffing crunch, name-line dilution, and the placements that make a real credential invisible. For status lines, out-of-province licences, and a before-and-after rewrite of a credentials block, see where to put your CPA, CFA, or P.Eng. on an academic CV.

The Short Answer: Where Professional Designations Go

Three placements, in priority order:

  • After your name. "Jane Smith, CPA, CMA" on the name line, capped at two or three current, relevant designations.
  • In a dedicated credentials block. A section called Professional Designations and Licensure, placed immediately after Education.
  • Nowhere else. Not buried in a job bullet, not under Honours and Awards, not only in the cover letter.

For teaching-stream and sessional roles in accredited professional programs, your designation is not decoration. It is a field somebody has to fill in. Where you put it determines whether they can find it, and if they can't find it, the field stays blank.

Let me explain what is actually happening on the other side of the submission portal.

The First Pass Is Not a Committee of Scholars

The image most industry applicants carry is five professors sitting around a table debating your file. That happens eventually, sometimes, for tenure-track searches.

For sessional and per-course appointments, the first pass is usually one person. An area coordinator, someone in the associate dean's office, or a department administrator working through a folder of PDFs. They are not holding a rubric for evaluating your intellectual contribution. They are holding a spreadsheet.

That spreadsheet has columns. Name. Highest degree. Designation. Status. Courses eligible to teach. Availability. The job in that sitting is sorting, not judging. Files that can be sorted move forward. Files that can't get set aside, and "set aside" during a compressed staffing window often means never revisited.

I want to be careful here. I have not seen a published Canadian study measuring how long that first-pass screen takes, and the "six-second recruiter scan" number you have seen online comes from corporate recruiting research, not academic hiring. So I won't dress up my experience as data. What I will tell you is that the process is mechanical, and mechanical processes reward retrievable facts. For how those pools actually get staffed, see how adjunct and sessional lecturer hiring works from the committee side.

The AACSB Qualification Matrix You Have Never Seen

If your target is an AACSB-accredited business school, there is a document behind that spreadsheet you have almost certainly never laid eyes on.

AACSB's business accreditation standards require schools to classify faculty into qualification categories and to document faculty sufficiency and qualifications. The categories are Scholarly Academic, Practice Academic, Scholarly Practitioner, and Instructional Practitioner. Professional certification and current professional practice feed into the practitioner categories.

Read that again. Your CPA is a reportable accreditation input. It is not a resume ornament. It is a data point the school will defend to a peer review team.

So when the area coordinator opens your file, their first real question is not "is this person a good teacher." It is "which category does this person land in, and does putting them in front of this section keep our qualification ratios where they need to be."

Engineering works on a parallel logic. Undergraduate engineering programs in Canada are accredited by the Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board, which publishes updated Accreditation Criteria and Procedures. Those criteria address the qualifications and licensure status of teaching staff, including expectations around P.Eng. licensure among those delivering engineering design and engineering science content, and the licensure of the head of the engineering unit. The specific wording changes between editions, so pull the current PDF from Engineers Canada rather than trusting anyone's summary, including mine.

Finance has a third version of this. CFA Institute runs a University Affiliation Program in which degree programs embed a substantial portion of the CFA Program Candidate Body of Knowledge. Affiliated programs have a documented interest in instructors who hold the charter.

Three different accreditation bodies, one shared mechanism: somebody has to record your credential status in a document that gets reviewed by outsiders.

The July Phone Call That Decides Sessional Hiring

Here is the moment where all of this stops being abstract.

It is mid-July. A required course in the fall timetable has no instructor. The person who was going to teach it took another appointment, or a sabbatical got approved late, or enrolment forced a second section. Someone opens the sessional pool and needs a qualified instructor by Friday.

In that moment, the most impressive CV in the pile loses to the most legible one. Nobody is reading page four to discover you are licensed. They are scanning for a credential that makes the course coverage defensible on the qualifications table, and they are scanning fast.

An eligibility-legible CV beats a more accomplished but illegible CV. That is the whole argument of this article.

One piece of context worth naming lightly. On January 22, 2024, IRCC announced a two-year cap on new study permit applications, expecting to approve roughly 360,000 study permits for 2024, about a 35 percent decrease from 2023. In September 2024 it announced a further reduction, targeting 437,000 study permits for 2025 and extending the cap into 2026. I won't overclaim what that has done to any specific department's budget. I will say that in my experience, tighter budgets have made per-course screening faster and more eligibility-driven, not more contemplative.

The Name Line: Post-Nominals on an Academic CV

Yes, put it after your name. This is where I part ways with a lot of generic advice.

The convention against post-nominals comes from research-faculty CV norms, where everyone in the pile has a PhD and the letters add no sorting information. That convention does not transfer cleanly to practice-track hiring, where the designation is the sorting information.

The rules, by body:

CFA. CFA Institute's trademark usage rules require the mark to be used as an adjective, never as a noun or a verb. Capital letters, no periods. "Jane Smith, CFA" is correct. "Jane Smith is a CFA" is not. Only charterholders in good standing may use it.

CPA. Provincially regulated, and each provincial body's bylaws and rules of professional conduct govern how the designation is displayed, including restrictions on use by suspended, resigned, or retired members. Check your own province's rules before you type it.

P.Eng. Engineering is a regulated profession across Canadian provinces and territories. The title is legally protected and may only be used by someone holding a current licence from the provincial or territorial regulator. Using it without a current licence is an offence under provincial engineering legislation. Include the province when it clarifies your jurisdiction.

Here is the part people underestimate. Your file may well be read by a committee member who holds the same designation you do. They know the trademark rules. They know what the bylaws say about inactive status. If you write "CFA Level II" after your name, which CFA Institute's rules do not permit, you have not signalled progress. You have signalled that you don't follow your own profession's rules, to a reader who does.

The dilution problem

An applicant with MBA, CPA, CFA, PMP, and a Six Sigma Black Belt puts all five after their name. The header now reads like a LinkedIn banner, and the two credentials that map to the course being staffed are competing for attention with three that don't.

Cap the name line at two, maybe three. Current. Regulated. Relevant to what you are being hired to teach. Everything else goes in a certifications block further down, where it can still be found without costing you signal at the top of page one.

The Credentials Block: A Dedicated Designations Section

Create a dedicated section. Call it Professional Designations and Licensure. Put it immediately after Education, before your teaching or professional experience.

Not inside Education. Education is degrees. A licence is a regulated status with a current standing, and it belongs where a screener checking eligibility expects to find a status.

Each entry should carry:

  • The designation as it is properly written
  • The full name spelled out, because not every reader knows every acronym
  • The issuing body
  • The province or jurisdiction
  • Year admitted
  • Current status
  • Registration or membership number, if the posting asks for it

Example of the shape:

CPA, CMA. Chartered Professional Accountant, Certified Management Accountant. CPA Ontario, admitted 2011. Status: good standing.

That entry takes two lines and fills four columns on somebody's spreadsheet. That is the entire point.

And keep licensure separate from memberships. Belonging to an association is not the same as holding a licence. Committees checking eligibility care about the licence. Memberships get their own lower section. For the rest of the document's order, see how to structure an academic CV section by section.

Where Designations Go to Die on a Canadian Academic CV

These are the placements that make a real credential functionally invisible:

Buried inside a job entry. A structural engineer with twenty years of consulting lists P.Eng. exactly once, in a bullet from 2011 that reads "promoted to Senior Engineer (P.Eng.)." A screener filling a licensure column will never find it.

Only in the cover letter. Cover letters frequently do not survive the first sort. Anything that determines eligibility belongs on the CV.

Filed under Honours and Awards. A charter is not a distinction. To a credentialed reader, this placement suggests you don't understand what your own designation is.

A naked acronym with no issuing body. If a reader has to look it up, it isn't doing its job.

Non-Canadian designations with no context. ACCA, CPA (US), Chartered Engineer, CA (India). List them properly with the issuing body and jurisdiction, and if you are pursuing Canadian recognition or a mutual recognition pathway, say so plainly on its own line. Silence gets read as a gap.

Mixing unregulated certifications into the licensure block. Your PMP and your P.Eng. are not the same category of thing, and putting them side by side under one heading undercuts both.

Designations in Progress, and One Honest Limitation

If you are working toward a designation, state it in plain language in the credentials block. Enrolled in the program. Passed a given level. What you cannot do, with CFA specifically, is construct a post-nominal that implies partial designation status. The rules allow you to state that you have passed a level or are enrolled in the program. They do not allow "CFA Level III" after your name.

Now the part most CV guides won't tell you.

For research-intensive tenure-track searches in non-professional units, your designation will not move the needle much. Those searches sort on publication record, grant history, and research trajectory. Putting CPA after your name on that application isn't wrong, but don't expect it to carry weight it was never built to carry.

The placement advice in this article is for practice-track hiring. Sessional, per-course, teaching-stream, professional programs with accreditation obligations. That is where the designation is eligibility, not flavour. If the prior question is whether a designation can substitute for a doctorate at all, start with how to get a university teaching job with a CPA or P.Eng.

One last thing. I have never seen evidence that PhD-holding committee members look down on professional designations, and I have sat with a lot of them. That fear shows up constantly in online threads. In accredited professional programs, the people reading your file are often relieved to find a qualified practitioner, because the alternative is a course with nobody in front of it in September.

If You Are Staring at a Corporate Resume

The summary statement, the skills bar, the one-page instinct, all of it has to go. But if you only make one edit before you submit, make it this one: move your designation to the name line and build the credentials block underneath Education. For the rest of that translation, see how hiring committees read an industry candidate's CV.

It is the highest-leverage twenty minutes you will spend on the document.
